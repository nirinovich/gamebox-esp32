1. The File Mystery: .SRT vs .STL

First Good News: You mentioned having an .SRT file. In digital media, an .srt file is a video subtitle file! In 3D printing, the universal format is .STL (Stereolithography / Standard Triangle Language), which contains the 3D surface mesh.

3D Model (.STL)

STAGE 3

Machine instructions file exported to SD card: coordinates, speeds, and extrusion volumes.
STAGE 4
Prusa i3 MK3S
Executes the G-code by heating to 215°C and laying molten plastic onto the heated PEI bed.

2. What You Must Know About the 0.4mm Nozzle ("Buse")

The 0.4 mm brass nozzle mounted on the E3D V6 hotend of your i3 MK3S is the gold standard of 3D printing. Understanding its physics prevents 95% of beginner mistakes:

  • The Extrusion Geometry: The nozzle opening is a 0.40 mm circle. When the direct-drive extruder pushes 1.75 mm filament through the 215°C heater block, the nozzle squishes the bead against the layer below, producing an extrusion width of approximately 0.45 mm.
  • The 75% Golden Rule of Layer Height: A bead of molten plastic must be pressed flat to bond with the layer beneath it. If your layer height is too high, the plastic bead stays round like spaghetti and fails to stick. Therefore, never exceed 75% of your nozzle diameter:
    0.40 mm × 0.75 = 0.30 mm max layer height.
  • The Sweet Spot for Cases (0.20mm QUALITY): For an ESP32-S3 case with screw posts, snap tabs, and USB-C port cutouts, 0.20 mm Quality gives tight dimensional tolerances and crisp overhangs while printing quickly.

3. Step-by-Step: Manipulating the Case in PrusaSlicer

Follow these exact steps to prepare your ESP32-S3 case:

  1. Set Up the Triple Presets (Top-Right Panel):
    Ensure these three dropdowns are selected:
    • Print settings: 0.20mm QUALITY
    • Filament: Prusament PLA or Generic PLA (PLA is rigid, odorless, and does not warp)
    • Printer: Original Prusa i3 MK3S & MK3S+ 0.4 nozzle
  2. Import your Model:
    Press Ctrl + I (or drag & drop ESP32-S3.stl from your Downloads folder directly onto the 3D grid).
  3. The Pro Move: "Place on Face" (F):
    3D models often import upside-down or tilted on an edge. In the left toolbar, click the Place on Face icon (or press F). Click the largest flat outer bottom surface of the case. The slicer will instantly snap the case perfectly flush with the heatbed!
  4. Wall Strength: Perimeters vs Infill:
    For electronics cases, strength comes from Perimeters (outer walls), not infill:
    • In the right panel, set Perimeters to 3 (giving ~1.35 mm solid plastic walls around your ESP32).
    • Set Infill to 15% with pattern Gyroid.
  5. Supports for USB-C & Port Cutouts:
    Set Supports: Everywhere. Under Print Settings > Support material, set Style: Organic. Organic (tree) supports branch delicately around the case and snap off cleanly with pliers without scarring your plastic!
  6. Slice and Inspect (Ctrl + G):
    Click the orange Slice now button at bottom-right. The screen switches to Preview mode. Drag the vertical orange scrollbar on the far right up and down to inspect how the nozzle will print each layer from 1 to finish.
  7. Export G-Code:
    Click Export G-code, save it directly onto your SD card, and safely eject the SD card.

4. Preparing the Physical Prusa i3 MK3S

The 2-Minute Pre-Flight Routine:

1. Clean the Bed: Pour a few drops of 90%+ Isopropyl Alcohol (IPA) on a paper towel and wipe the smooth PEI spring steel sheet while it is cold. Finger oils prevent plastic from sticking!

2. Insert SD Card & Start: Insert the SD card on the left side of the LCD screen. Turn the silver knob to Print from SD, click to select your ESP32-S3 file.

3. The Crucial First Layer (Live Z): Stay by the printer during the first 2 minutes. The printer will perform 9-point mesh bed leveling, heat the nozzle to 215°C and bed to 60°C, and lay down the first layer. The lines should be slightly squished and completely smooth. If lines look like round noodles or peel up, adjust Live Z down slightly using the knob.
